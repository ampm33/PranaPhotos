The Concept

What & Why

PranaPhotos is a multi-page photography portfolio built entirely with semantic HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript — no frameworks, no templates. The site serves as both a client-facing booking platform and a living demonstration of front-end development skills developed over a full academic quarter.

The concept centers on a single question: how do you make photography feel felt, not just seen? The answer was restraint — a high-contrast black and white foundation that lets images breathe, paired with a warm gold accent system that nods to the brand identity. Every layout decision was backed by usability testing, accessibility audits, and iterative feedback across eight sprint releases.

What I Learned

Reflection

This project taught me that clean design does not automatically equal accessible design. Visually, the site felt minimal and organized from the start — but structurally, it needed real work to meet professional standards.

The biggest shift in my thinking was learning to treat the browser as a collaborative medium: semantic HTML isn't just syntax, it's a set of promises to the user — about navigation, hierarchy, and meaning. Building every page by hand, from the spacing tokens to the IntersectionObserver reveal logic, made those promises feel earned.

01
Systems Thinking Replacing arbitrary px values with an 8pt token system (--sp-1 through --sp-10) made every spacing decision consistent and defensible.
02
User Testing Changes Everything Lab 8.2 revealed that the Prints section sparked genuine interest — something I hadn't anticipated. Testing surfaced what no amount of solo review could.
03
Accessibility is Structure From Lab 4.2 to Lab 8.3, the Lighthouse score went from 6.7 to 9.4. Every point was earned through semantics, contrast, and keyboard navigation — not visual polish.
04
Brand Identity Runs Deep The logo, the gold, the Times New Roman — when brand tokens are consistent across every page, the site stops feeling like a project and starts feeling like a business.
